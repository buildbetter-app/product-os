# Map Customer Journey

## Contract

Map what customers actually do, including off-product and human steps. Keep the current-state journey separate from the desired future state. Avoid decorative maps without evidence or owners.

## Workflow

1. Define the journey's actor, segment, start trigger, end outcome, and scope boundary.
2. Gather behavioral evidence across calls, observation, product data, support, surveys, and service teams.
3. Lay out stages in customer language. For each, record job, action, touchpoint, expectation, emotion, friction, workaround, handoff, and evidence.
4. Mark moments that disproportionately affect trust, conversion, activation, retention, or cost.
5. Separate observed problems from hypothesized causes. Note segment and role differences rather than averaging incompatible journeys.
6. Prioritize opportunities by customer impact, frequency, strategic relevance, and control.
7. Assign measures and an owner to each selected opportunity; capture gaps requiring further research.

## BuildBetter Acceleration

If BuildBetter MCP is available, search organization skills first. Use calls, signals, survey responses, people properties, documents, and projects to triangulate each stage. Preserve IDs and date ranges; do not treat internal descriptions as observed customer behavior.

## Output

Return scope, actor and segment, current-state journey table, key moments, evidence gaps, prioritized opportunities, measures, owners, and a clearly labeled future-state sketch when requested.

## Quality Gate

Every stage needs evidence or an explicit hypothesis label. Reject a map that starts and ends at product screens when the customer job extends beyond them.
