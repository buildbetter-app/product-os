# Define Product Vision

## Contract

Describe a future worth creating, not a release plan, slogan, revenue target, or list of features. The vision should guide tradeoffs while surviving multiple strategies and implementations.

## Workflow

1. Ground in the organization's purpose, customer evidence, market context, current product, and durable constraints.
2. Name the primary beneficiary and the future change in their behavior, capability, or outcome.
3. Explain why that future matters and why this organization can credibly contribute.
4. Define the product's distinctive role and the principles that shape how it creates value.
5. State boundaries and non-goals so the vision rejects attractive distractions.
6. Write a concise vision narrative, then test it against plausible strategic choices: it must help choose.
7. Mark evidence, hypotheses, and unknowns; establish a review trigger based on changed reality, not a calendar alone.

## BuildBetter Acceleration

If BuildBetter MCP is available, search organization skills first. Use customer evidence, knowledge pages, documents, project history, and survey responses to ground the current reality and desired change. State what BuildBetter sources were searched.

## Output

Return the vision statement, future narrative, beneficiary, distinctive role, principles, boundaries, evidence and assumptions, decision tests, and review triggers.

## Quality Gate

Remove dates, features, implementation, generic superlatives, and metrics better suited to strategy. The vision must be ambitious, comprehensible, and capable of saying no.
